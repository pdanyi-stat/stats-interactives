● Module 6 · From samples to populations

Sampling distributions.

We rarely get to see a whole population. Instead we see one sample and compute its mean. On this page you will draw thousands of samples from real county election returns and watch where the sample means land. You will see that they center on the truth, and that they cluster more tightly as the samples grow.

Check yourself

      

        At n = 25 the standard error of Biden share is about 3.2 points. What will it be at n = 100?
        
It will be about 1.6 points. The standard error is σ/√n, so quadrupling n divides it by √4 = 2. Precision grows with the square root of the sample size, which is why polls with 4,000 respondents are not four times better than polls with 1,000.

      

      

        The population of county vote shares is right-skewed. What shape is the sampling distribution at n = 50?
        
It will be close to normal. The Central Limit Theorem says the distribution of sample means approaches a normal curve as n grows, whatever shape the population has. Its center is still μ.

      

      

        Why does Total votes cast need a much larger n before the bell shape appears?
        
A few enormous counties sit far out in the right tail. A small sample that happens to include one of them gets a huge mean. The more extreme the skew, the larger n has to be before the Central Limit Theorem takes hold.

      

      

        Biden won about 51% of the national vote. Why is μ only about 33%?
        
The unit of analysis is the county, and every county counts once. Loving County, Texas (66 votes) weighs as much as Los Angeles County. Most counties are small, rural and Republican. Always ask what one row of the data represents.

Formulas

    
  

  

    
Population mean and standard deviation

    μ=1N∑i=1Nxiσ=1N∑i=1N(xi−μ)2
    
These are computed once from all N = 3,112 counties, while in real research they are the unknowns.

  

  

    
Sample mean

    x‾=1n∑i=1nxi
    
The sample mean is a statistic, so it changes from sample to sample, and panel C collects those changing values.

  

  

    
Center of the sampling distribution

    E(x‾)=μ
    
The sample mean is unbiased. On average it hits the population mean exactly.

  

  

    
Standard error of the mean

    SE(x‾)=σn
    
This is the standard deviation of the sampling distribution. If you quadruple n, the SE is cut in half. In practice σ is unknown, so we plug in the sample SD, s.

  

  

    
Central Limit Theorem

    x‾∼approx.N(μ,σn)
    
For large enough n the sample mean is approximately normal, whatever shape the population has. Heavier skew needs a larger n.

  

  

    
Proportions (a 0/1 variable)

    p^=x‾SE(p^)=p(1−p)n
    
For a 1/0 variable the mean is the share of 1s, and σ = √(p(1−p)).

  

  

    
Finite population correction

    SE=σnN−nN−1
    
Sampling without replacement from a finite population shrinks the SE slightly. With N = 3,112 and n ≤ 100 the factor is at least 0.984, so this page ignores it.
