● Module 3 · Describing data

Skew and kurtosis.

There are two ways to compare a variable's distribution with the normal curve. Skew asks whether it is lopsided, and kurtosis asks whether it is peaked or flat. The examples use the Titanic passenger list, the 2004 ANES feeling thermometers, GDP per capita for 177 countries and the Massachusetts counties.

Part 1

Which way does the tail point?

A skewed distribution has a fat end and a tail. If the tail points toward the low values, the skew is negative, and if it points toward the high values, the skew is positive. For comparison, the dashed curve is a normal curve with the same mean and SD.

Which way is the brontosaurus's tail pointing?

Picture the distribution as a brontosaurus, where the big hump is its body and the long thin stretch is its tail. The tail points the way of the skew.

Statistical software gives skewness as a number, where 0 means no skew, a value above 0 means positive skew, and a value below 0 means negative skew.

Part 2

Skew pulls the mean away from the median

Module 2 introduced the mean as the balance point and the median as the middle case. In a skewed distribution the two come apart, because the few cases in the long tail drag the mean toward them while the median stays with the bulk of the cases.

Part 3

Stretch a tail, raise a peak

Kurtosis describes how peaked or flat a distribution is compared with the normal curve. A tall, narrow peak is leptokurtic (kurtosis above 0). A flat, wide one is platykurtic (below 0). The first slider leans the curve, and the second raises or flattens the peak. Watch the mean (orange) and median (blue) as the curve leans, and you will see the long tail pull the mean away from the peak, just as it did in Part 2.

Wrap-up

What we learned

  • Skew describes which way the long tail points. A tail toward high values is positive skew, and a tail toward low values is negative skew.
  • A long tail pulls the mean toward it, while the median stays with most of the cases.
  • Kurtosis describes how peaked or flat a distribution is compared with the normal curve. Above 0 is a tall, narrow peak (leptokurtic), and below 0 is a flat, wide one (platykurtic).
  • An average alone can hide a divided public, so we check the shape of a distribution before we trust its mean.
