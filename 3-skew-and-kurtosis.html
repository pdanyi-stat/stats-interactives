● Module 3 · Describing data

Skew and kurtosis.

There are two ways to compare a variable's distribution with the normal curve. Skew asks whether it is lopsided, and kurtosis asks whether it is peaked or flat. The examples use the Titanic passenger list, the 2004 ANES feeling thermometers, GDP per capita for 177 countries and the Massachusetts counties.

Part 1

Which way does the tail point?

A skewed distribution has a fat end and a tail. If the tail points toward the low values, the skew is negative, and if it points toward the high values, the skew is positive. For comparison, the dashed curve is a normal curve with the same mean and SD.

Which way is the brontosaurus's tail pointing?

Picture the distribution as a brontosaurus, where the big hump is its body and the long thin stretch is its tail. The tail points the way of the skew.

SPSS gives skewness as a number, where 0 means no skew, a value above 0 means positive skew, and a value below 0 means negative skew.

Part 2

Stretch a tail, raise a peak

Kurtosis describes how peaked or flat a distribution is compared with the normal curve. A tall, narrow peak is leptokurtic (kurtosis above 0). A flat, wide one is platykurtic (below 0). The first slider leans the curve, and the second raises or flattens the peak. Watch the mean (orange) and median (blue) as the curve leans, and you will see the long tail pull the mean away from the peak.

Skew pulls the mean away from the median
