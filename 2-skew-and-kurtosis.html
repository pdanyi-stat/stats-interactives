Skew and kurtosis

Two ways to compare a variable's distribution with the normal curve: is it lopsided (skew), and is it peaked or flat (kurtosis)? The examples use the Titanic passenger list, the 2004 ANES feeling thermometers, GDP per capita for 177 countries and the Massachusetts counties.

Part 1

Which way does the tail point?

A skewed distribution has a fat end and a tail. If the tail points toward the low values, the skew is negative; toward the high values, positive. The dashed curve is a normal curve with the same mean and SD, for comparison.

Which way is the brontosaurus's tail pointing?

Turn the distribution into a brontosaurus: the big hump is its body, and the long thin stretch is its tail. The tail points the way of the skew.

ObservationsNormal curve (dashed)
The tail points

SPSS gives skewness as a number: 0 = no skew, above 0 = positive skew, below 0 = negative skew.

Part 2

Stretch a tail, raise a peak

Kurtosis describes how peaked or flat a distribution is compared with the normal curve. A tall, narrow peak is leptokurtic (kurtosis above 0). A flat, wide one is platykurtic (below 0). The first slider leans the curve; the second raises or flattens the peak. Watch the mean (orange) and median (blue) as the curve leans: the long tail pulls the mean away from the peak.
