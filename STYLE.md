# Writing rules for this site

These rules govern every word of copy on the site: page introductions, card text, captions, feedback messages, notes and buttons that hold a sentence. They are strict. Check every new or edited sentence against them before it ships.

## 1. Banned sentence forms

1. **The tacked-on fragment.** No sentence that ends in a comma followed by a phrase with no verb of its own.
   - Wrong: "Play the guessing game three ways, at three levels of measurement."
   - Wrong: "Ten women and ten men, drawn at random from the 2004 NES."
   - Right: "We will play the same guessing game three times, once for each level of measurement."
2. **Fragments used as sentences.** Every sentence needs a subject and a main verb. No "Same game, bigger tables." and no "Nominal Y. Guess the most common category."
3. **The colon reveal.** No colon that sets up a punchline or answer: "They answer one question: …", "Your job: …", "The strategy never changes: …". A colon may only introduce a list, a formula, an SPSS menu path, or a label in a key or a table.
4. **Comma splices.** Two complete sentences may not be joined by a comma. Use a period, or join them with a word such as "and", "but", "so" or "because".
5. **Stacked short sentences for effect.** No runs of three or more short declarative sentences, and no slogan-like pairs such as "Same X, different Y." or "Not X. Y."
6. **Clipped semicolon pairs.** No "0 means X doesn't help at all; 1 means no errors left." Write the two ideas as connected sentences.
7. **Headings** may be short phrases, but they may not use the forms above ("Start simple: …", "Same game, bigger tables").

## 2. Write the way Paul writes his lab handouts

The model is his own course documents in Drive: *Describing Attributes of Data Notes*, *Strength of Relationships (Lambda, Gamma)*, and *Comparison of Means and Hypothesis Testing*. Read them again before a large rewrite. What they do:

1. **Full, connected sentences that explain why.** Ideas are linked with words like "so", "since", "because", "then", "that suggests", "meaning", "recall that", rather than chopped into fragments.
   - From the handouts: "PRE measures make a prediction about the value of the dependent variable without knowing the independent variable. Subsequently, we make a prediction about the dependent variable with knowledge of the independent variable."
2. **"We" for what the class does together, "you" for what the student does.**
   - "Let's take up lambda as an example so as to introduce it and walk through the calculation."
   - "You need, of course, to know how your grouping variable is coded, so be sure to check."
3. **Set up the data before the task.** Say where the data come from, what each variable measures, how it is coded and which way it runs, which variable is the IV and which is the DV, and only then what to do.
   - "The IV, partisan vote in 2000, runs left to right from Strong Bush States to Strong Gore States, so moving right means a state is more blue."
4. **Define a term in plain words the first time, with a short gloss in parentheses.**
   - "the dependent variable (the outcome) and the independent variable (the potential cause)"
5. **Give SPSS steps by their menu names, in quotes, in the order you click them.**
6. **Interpret in "real world language."** After a number, say what it means about the people, states or countries.
7. **A quick version, then a longer one, is fine.** "Here is the quick and dirty way to interpret." followed by "Now for a slightly longer explanation."
8. **Dry asides are allowed, sparingly.** "[pardon the term, it was the language of the era and the dataset]", "place your best Karl Marx hat on". Never slogans, hype, or exclamation-heavy copy.
9. **Plain and serious overall.** The copy should sound like a professor talking a class through a handout, not like marketing.

## 3. Standing content rules

- Say "reject" or "fail to reject" the null hypothesis. Never "accept".
- Use "SD", not "s.d." or "std. dev." in prose.
- Every number must be real and traceable to the data or a cited source. Never invent an output.
- No aunt-and-uncle framing unless Paul asks for it.
- No language about exams or grading. The site is for learning.
- Use the AHCA final exam only as a model, never as data.
- Ask before adding content beyond what was agreed.

## 4. Check before shipping

For every sentence you add or change:

1. Does it have a subject and a main verb?
2. Does it end in a comma plus a verbless phrase? Rewrite it.
3. Does a colon set up a reveal? Rewrite it.
4. Are two complete sentences joined by a comma? Split or join them properly.
5. Would it sound natural read aloud in Paul's handouts? If not, rewrite it in that voice.
